Episode 0 · The Baseline

Grid Code Compliance and the
Future of Data Centre Design

The grid used to be a supplier. Now it is a connection counterparty with explicit performance requirements, and that changes how facilities are designed.

The Shift

Why data centre design changed

For decades, the design brief was simple: protect the information technology load. When grid voltage looked unhealthy, transfer to uninterruptible power supply and generator sets. That worked at modest scale. It stopped working when individual sites reached hundreds of megawatts.

Then

Protect the load

Internal reliability only. Tier ratings, ITIC power acceptability curves, backup generator sets. Grid-side behaviour was outside the facility scope.

The trigger

Scale and real events

Facilities now reach hundreds of megawatts to gigawatt scale. In July 2024, approximately 1.5 GW of data centre load disconnected during a 230 kV reclosing sequence when facilities transferred to backup (North American Electric Reliability Corporation Large Loads Task Force, 2025).

Now

Support the grid

Grid operators are setting ride-through, recovery, and cycling requirements. There is no global standard; each operator sets its own numbers.

In short: ride-through used to mean the load rides on backup. It now means the facility rides through on the grid.

Staying connected is not passive

Data centres tend toward constant-power draw. When voltage sags, current rises at the worst moment for the grid. The facility must stay connected and limit what it draws. The shortfall must be supplied locally.

The Words

What the grid is now asking for

Terms used in connection studies and grid codes. Most were absent from data centre design briefs until large-load requirements emerged.

Voltage Ride-Through

Stay connected through voltage sags and interruptions deeper and longer than information technology equipment alone tolerates.

Frequency Ride-Through

Stay connected through frequency excursions outside the continuous operating band, not voltage events alone.

Active Power Recovery

After the fault clears, return to at least 90% of pre-fault active power within the operator's recovery window.

Cyclic load

Artificial intelligence training can swing tens to hundreds of megawatts on second-to-second cycles (Microsoft/NVIDIA, 2025). The grid sees this as forced oscillation.

Sub-Synchronous Torsional Interaction

Those oscillations can resonate with synchronous generator shafts, amplifying torsional stress (Energy Systems Integration Group study case, 2026).

Ramp limits

Limit how fast demand changes, especially at 100 MW scale and above.

Every Site Is Different

No global standard

Requirements converge in kind but differ in numbers. Your site choice carries a compliance profile.

So What

What this means for your project

Gates connection

Demonstrate compliance by simulation before energisation. Connection studies gate the project schedule.

Reshapes the single-line diagram

Ride-through, recovery, and load smoothing become design inputs, not late-stage additions.

Affects procurement

Equipment that closes these gaps carries its own lead times and cost.
