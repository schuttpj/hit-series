Current harmonics
in grid-connected
data centres

Nonlinear loads draw nonsinusoidal currents from the grid. The harmonic content of those currents at the point of common coupling is subject to limits under IEEE 519-2022. Facilities that exceed those limits are non-compliant at the meter.

The Problem

Where harmonics come from

Harmonics are created when nonlinear loads draw nonsinusoidal current from a sinusoidal voltage source. The distorted current waveform can be decomposed by Fourier analysis into a fundamental component and a series of harmonic components at integer multiples of the supply frequency.

A six-pulse rectifier, the topology used in most UPS systems and the front end of many adjustable speed drives, draws a quasi-square wave line current rather than a sinusoidal one. For an ideal six-pulse rectifier with a large load inductance approximating a current source, the line current has a total harmonic distortion of 31% and contains no triplen harmonics. The characteristic harmonic orders present are h = 6k ± 1 (k = 1, 2, 3, …): the 5th, 7th, 11th, 13th, 17th, 19th, and so on.

Under the assumption of a current-source load, the amplitude of each characteristic harmonic relative to the fundamental is Ih / I1 = 1/h. In practice, finite line inductance and commutation overlap reduce the higher-order amplitudes below the 1/h envelope.

Variable-frequency drives add further harmonic content through their own six-pulse input rectifier stages. The combined loading of multiple nonlinear loads in a data centre determines the total harmonic current presented to the utility at the point of common coupling (PCC).

Compliance Limit

IEEE 519-2022: harmonic current limits at the PCC

IEEE 519 sets limits on the harmonic currents that a customer may inject into the utility system at the point of common coupling. The limits are indexed by the short-circuit ratio ISC / IL, which is the ratio of the maximum short-circuit current available at the PCC to the maximum demand load current at the fundamental frequency.

The measure of compliance is Total Demand Distortion (TDD): the root-sum-square of all harmonic current components from order 2 to 50, expressed as a percentage of the maximum demand load current IL at the PCC.

TDD = √(Σ Ih2) / IL × 100%

TDD differs from Total Harmonic Distortion (THD). THD normalises to the fundamental component I1. TDD normalises to the maximum demand current IL. At part load, IL remains fixed at its maximum demand value, so TDD is lower than THD for the same harmonic amplitudes.

For the ERCOT reference design simulation (ISC / IL = 17.4, tier ISC/IL < 20), the applicable limits are: TDD ≤ 5%, with individual order limits of 4.0% for h < 11, 2.0% for 11 ≤ h < 17, and 1.5% for 17 ≤ h < 23.

The short-circuit ratio ISC / IL characterises the stiffness of the supply relative to the load. A lower ratio indicates a larger load relative to the available fault current, so tighter harmonic limits apply. For ISC/IL < 20, the 5% TDD limit represents the most restrictive tier in the standard.

HIT Response

Harmonic current compensation via the HIT shunt converter

The HIT shunt converter (VSC2) injects a compensation current into the circuit node between the series HIT transformer and the load. That injected current cancels the harmonic content of the load current before it reaches the PCC.

The nonlinear load draws a distorted current containing a fundamental component and harmonic components. VSC2, connected in shunt at the load node, measures the load current and injects a compensation current equal and opposite to the harmonic content:

icomp = − iharm

The harmonic content and the compensation current cancel at the shunt node. The current in the series HIT path toward the PCC then contains only the fundamental component. This is the mechanism described in Kusko & Thompson (p. 96): "The active compensator senses the harmonic current and injects a compensation current, Ic, which cancels the harmonic current. The net supply current contains only the fundamental."

In the HDT topology described by Doff-Sotta et al., VSC2 is the load-side converter that "manages shunt current injection." Harmonic mitigation is one of several ancillary services VSC2 can provide alongside reactive power compensation and load phase balancing.

In the Reference Design June 2026 v1, the shunt current reference output of the OEM firmware is rated to ±1 530 A peak, set by the power electronics dimensioning of the build.

1
Nonlinear load draws distorted current

The load current contains the fundamental at 60 Hz plus harmonic components at h = 5, 7, 11, 13, … with amplitudes following approximately the 1/h envelope for the six-pulse topology.

2
OEM firmware extracts the harmonic reference

The OEM controller, running at 20 kHz, samples the load current and computes the harmonic current reference signal iharm that VSC2 must cancel.

3
VSC2 injects the compensation current

VSC2 injects icomp = −iharm at the shunt node. The harmonic components in the load current and the injected compensation current cancel. Only the fundamental current continues toward the PCC through the series HIT path.

4
PCC current meets the IEEE 519 limit

TDD at the metering point is reduced from the uncompensated value toward the 5% limit. How close the result comes to zero TDD depends on the current rating and control bandwidth of VSC2.
